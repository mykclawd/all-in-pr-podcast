# Host personas

Write each host by role and speech pattern. Light running bits are fine
("the besties", "Rain Man"); keep them to one or two per episode so the
technical content wins.

## Jason (moderator)

- Job: drives the episode, asks the questions a smart non-author would ask,
  keeps time, recaps.
- Voice: high energy, salesy, quick. Opens hot, cuts people off to move on.
- Patterns: "Okay, okay, let's get into it." "Friedberg, break it down for
  us." "Wait, so you're telling me..." "Let's go to Sacks, because I know
  you hate this." "Love you, besties."
- Rule: every Jason question pushes the episode to the next beat. He asks for
  the "why" explicitly and asks one clarifying question during the "how".

## Friedberg (the technician)

- Job: explains exactly how the PR works. Owns most of the airtime.
- Voice: calm, precise, a bit professorial. Builds the explanation step by
  step, like walking through a lab protocol.
- Patterns: "So mechanically, here's what happens." "Step one... then..."
  "The key detail is..." "Under the hood..." "To be precise..."
- Rule: names real files, functions, flags, and numbers in data-flow order.
  Every claim comes from the claims ledger. Corrects others if they
  oversimplify.
- Owns the ELI5 analogy: one everyday picture that maps step for step onto
  the real mechanism, then he refers back to it during the deep dive.

## Chamath (what actually matters)

- Job: interrupts to call out the one or two highest-leverage facts: the thing
  that will bite you, the thing that makes this scale, the number to remember.
- Voice: direct, emphatic, first-principles, a bit grandiose.
- Patterns: "Let me just say something, because this is really important."
  "This is the whole ballgame." "If you remember one thing..." "The second-order
  effect here is..."
- Rule: 2-3 turns, short. Each one restates a key fact in a sharper way.
  Never introduces new technical claims Friedberg hasn't set up.

## Sacks (devil's advocate)

- Job: surfaces the trade-offs by attacking the most debatable decision and
  naming the alternative that wasn't chosen.
- Voice: dry, lawyerly, skeptical, confident. Builds an argument like a
  closing statement.
- Patterns: "Look, I'm going to push back here." "Let's be honest about what
  this actually costs." "The steelman for the other approach is..." "I'm not
  saying it's wrong, I'm saying you're paying for it."
- Rule: at least two turns. One must name a concrete alternative and its
  upside; another must name a concrete risk (perf, complexity, migration,
  coupling, failure mode). Stays fair: if the PR's choice is right, he
  concedes with a condition ("Fine, as long as...").
